> questions from data and by deciding *how* the tiers actually work. Reasoned through with Fab

## 1. The data that reframes the ladder

Measured on **1646 real first-prompts** from Fab's own coding corpus (the RFC-005 SFT dataset, via
`freecode-classify`):

| class | share |
|---|---|
| codegen | 55.5% |
| **audit** (comprehension/review) | **30.1%** |
| output-distill | 13.7% |
| chat-qa / unknown | <1% |

So **~86% of real work is "heavy" (codegen + audit)** and is T2-bound; only ~14% is light. The
economic case for tiering by *model size* is therefore thin: even with a small model, it would only
serve the 14% briciole. **The tiers must be split by the NATURE of the work, not its size.**

## 2. The two battles inside the heavy 86%

The 86% is **not uniformly hard** (Fab's framing). It splits into two battles:

- **Battle 1 — structural / AST-compliant.** Tree transformations where the *shape of the result is
  constrained by the existing structure*: rename→update call-sites, add field→update constructors,
  change signature, implement a trait given its definition, test scaffold derived from a signature,
  `unwrap()`→`?`. The model does **pattern-completion on tree shapes**, not reasoning. We should win
  this **because the AST is the ground truth** — `syn`/`prettyplease`/`ApplyAstEdit`/analyzers are
  already half this machinery.
- **Battle 2 — transient.** Where the answer *is not in the tree*: "it's flaky, find out why", "what
  is the right abstraction", a non-local bug that only reproduces in prod. Needs reasoning,
  hypotheses, world-knowledge. **This stays with the frontier model (T2 / Claude).**

## 3. Decision: generate-and-validate (the harder path)

The transformation is **generated by the model and only VALIDATED by the AST/gates** — *not*
pattern-instantiated (model picks a known refactor → deterministic code applies it). Why the harder
path is the right one:

- **Generality.** Pattern-instantiation caps you at your hand-coded catalog; generate-and-validate
  covers any transformation that passes the validator — including ones you never anticipated.
- **Bitter lesson.** Hand-engineered features don't scale; a generator + a deterministic verifier
  does (it is the generator/verifier paradigm — enables best-of-N / repair).
- **Matches the data.** The 1646 trajectories are free-form edits, not pattern instances.
- **The gates make free generation safe** — freecode's whole thesis. A weak/cheap tier may *attempt*
  freely because deterministic gates catch its mistakes and escalate.

> **Consequence that flips the roadmap:** the near-term, load-bearing work is the **VALIDATOR, not
> the model.** Build the validator now (model-free), calibrate it on T2 (Claude's edits), and freelm
> slots in behind a gate you already trust.

## 4. The validator stack (the layered ground truth)

"Validated by the AST" must **not** mean "parses" — syntactically-valid-but-wrong code passes a
parse check. Free generation is only as safe as the weakest accepted layer. The validator is a
**stack of deterministic filters**:

```
parse / AST-valid → structural invariants → type-check → compile → safety/slop gates → affected tests green
```

**The escalation boundary is measured, not classified.** You cannot tell hard-codegen from
easy-codegen from the prompt. So T1 *attempts*; the stack arbitrates. **Failure to find a
structurally-valid, gate-passing transformation in K tries *is* the signal it was a Battle-2
(reasoning) problem → escalate to T2.** The validation failure-rate is the router's input.

## 5. The three tiers — by EFFORT, swap-in by model

The key unlock: tiers need not be three *models*. They are three **effort levels of one model now**,
with the model-swap (freelm into T1) as a later drop-in.

| tier | what | who | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T0** | deterministic / no model (RFC-003 compression, the greeting short-circuit) | "ciao", output-distill, formatting | ~0 |
| **T1** | model + **minimal** scaffold (compressed context, no tool-loop, one-shot); freelm later | distill, qa, *routine* codegen/audit | low |
| **T2** | model + **full** scaffold (full context, tool-loop, gates, retries) | the genuinely hard work | full |

Escalation rungs: **T0→T1** (the "light" label was wrong, it needs files); **T1→T2** (a gate vetoes,
or N retries exhausted). Today T1 and T2 are the same endpoint at different effort; freelm becomes
T1 with no architecture change. **The prize is moving *routine-heavy* work off the frontier model —
not the 14% light stuff.**

## 6. The #1 risk and the verdict firewall

**Fab's pinned #1 risk: the closed validation loop** — code written, audited, and tested by the same
loop / same model family waves through correlated errors. Orthogonal evidence is mandatory.

The verdict is therefore a **hard AND-chain with a typed firewall**, never a fuzzy belief:

- **Hard signals = binary veto, un-overridable** (`freecode-verdict::HardVerdict`, which has **no
  numeric/LogOdds constructor by design** — a veto is type-unrepresentable as a weighable term):
  - compiler / type-check result (`run_compile_check`)
  - regression seesaw (`passed = regressed.is_empty()`)
  - `safety_gate` Severity::Error (secrets, merge markers, HIDDEN_CHARS/bidi, injection hits)
  - blast-radius / HITL tier (`classify_tier == FullAccess`)
- **Soft signals = weighable** (only these may ever be aggregated): a T1/freelm self-rating, an
  SLM/LLM-judge score, `safety_gate` Warn/Info, analyzer findings below Error.

`Ship` is reachable **only when every hard gate passes**. The soft layer can never overturn a veto.
(Property-tested: a single `Veto` cannot `Ship` under any pile of passes at any retry count.)

**Shipped (2026-06-22):** `freecode-verdict` crate (the typed firewall + `route()` → {Ship,
RetrySameTier, EscalateToT2}) — adversarially verified airtight — and **telemetry-only** 3-way
router logging in `escalation.rs::log_route` (drives nothing; measures how often the
"uncertain→escalate" band would fire). Soft aggregation is deliberately **absent** (see §7).

## 7. AIMP as the glue — the decision (C / A / B)

Can [AIMP](https://github.com/fabriziosalmi/aimp) (Fab's Merkle-CRDT + epistemic-layer mesh
protocol) be the glue between tiers/validators? Grounded, adversarial answer:

**What genuinely fits (only L3, the epistemic *algebra*):**
- **`aggregate_correlated` CorrelationCell discounting** — the *one* load-bearing fit: it turns
  "an edit audited by the same model family is weak evidence" from a slogan into arithmetic (N
  correlated "looks fine" votes → ~1.42× evidence, not N×). It is the quantitative form of the #1 risk.
- **integer LogOdds** as a deterministic, float-free graded-confidence type for soft signals.
- **the 3-way thresholded classifier** (accept / uncertain / reject) → ship / escalate / retry — gives
  the router its missing "uncertain → escalate" signal.

**What is over-engineering *here* (distributed-only, inert at N=1):** the whole network — Merkle-CRDT
merge, quorum/BFT, Ed25519 signing, equivocation/deny-list, Sybil-resistant reputation, vector
clocks, and v0.4 SimHash auto-edges. Inside one trusted daemon there is no partition, no adversary,
no Sybil surface. Citing the convergence/quorum theorems against an in-process `for`-loop is a
category error. (They become real only when validators run as **separate, mutually-distrusting
daemons across a fleet**.)

**Three corrections the adversarial pass surfaced (one is a fix to AIMP itself):**
1. The CorrelationCell must key on **correlated FAILURE-MODE** ("is this a language-model
   judgment?"), **not** `model_family_id` (AIMP's own suggestion) — keying on family fires on the
   harmless self-vote and *misses* the dangerous cross-family shared-prior agreement.
2. The discount is **sign-symmetric but the risk is asymmetric**: discounting *slows* compounding,
   it does not *bound* a confident self-accept. Scope it to push borderline cases toward *escalate*,
   never to *certify* acceptance.
3. AIMP's v0.4 contradiction-damping ("one Contradicts can't flip Accepted→Rejected") is **actively
   wrong for hard gates** — a single `rustc` Error must flip the verdict unconditionally. The
   property AIMP is proud of is the opposite of what a compiler gate needs → hard gates stay outside
   the algebra entirely (the typed firewall).

**The decision:**
- **C — now:** keep the hard-gate AND-chain; against the #1 risk, simply **do not admit a
  same-family self-validator into the verdict** — structural exclusion, *one `if`*. Absence of the
  correlated voter is a *stronger* guarantee than discounting it to 1.42×. (RFC-004 §5 Q2 also says
  defer self-rating.)
- **A — at the trigger:** the moment a model-family-correlated soft voter is introduced (freelm
  rates its own edit, or an SLM juror enters the verdict), lift **~200 LoC of pure L3 algebra**
  (LogOdds + `aggregate_correlated` + classifier), behind the typed firewall — and *only* then.
- **B — never, until distributed:** AIMP-the-network earns its theorems only if freecode fans out to
  an org-wide mesh of independent validator daemons.

**Smallest builds that test the idea (no AIMP import, no freelm):** (1) the 3-way router telemetry
over existing gates — *shipped*; (2) a standalone `freecode-verdict` crate whose one property test
proves correlated same-cell agreement caps at ~1.42× *while* a single hard `Veto` stays
un-overridable — partially shipped (the firewall half; the soft half is gated on A).

## 8. Connection to freelm (RFC-005)

**T1 = freelm.** The RFC-005 SFT dataset (1646 conversations, mode/task_class-tagged) is the T1
generator's training data; a **Battle-1-specific export** (AST `before → after` transformation pairs
from the Edit/Write trajectories) narrows freelm's task to "given AST context + intent, produce the
tree edit" — far more learnable than general coding. **freelm's existence is the trigger for decision
A** (the soft algebra). Battle 2 stays with Claude and is never imitated.
